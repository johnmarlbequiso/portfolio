How I think about offers

The math behind every yes

Desired Outcome × Perceived Likelihood Effort & Sacrifice × Time Delay
Desired Outcome ↑
The dream result they actually want, not the feature I'm offering.
Perceived Likelihood ↑
How much they believe it will actually work for them, specifically.
Effort & Sacrifice ↓
What it costs them in time, money, or hassle to get there.
Time Delay ↓
How long they have to wait before they see it working.

Nobody buys a service. They buy a faster, more certain path to a result they already want.

Push the top up. Make the outcome bigger. Make it believable.

Push the bottom down. Cut the effort. Cut the wait.

Do both, and price stops being the objection. Do neither, and no script saves the call.

Method

The frameworks behind it

S.C.I.
Surface → what's visible on the profile.
Context → what it means for their business.
Insight → the connection most people miss.
A.C.A.
Acknowledge → a specific, real detail.
Compliment → genuine, not flattery.
Ask → low-pressure, two-option question.
Closing Flow
Rapport → create safety before anything else.
Agenda → set direction, remove awkwardness.
Qualify → find the real pain (longest part).
Close → one direct question, right timing.
Objections → loop back to Qualify, close again.
Objection Formula
Acknowledge → "I completely understand."
Clarify → "What makes you feel that way?"
Reframe → "That's exactly why this matters."
Buyer Response Triggers
Relevance → "This applies to me."
Credibility → "This person seems real."
Curiosity → "Let me check this out."
Clarity → "I know what to do next."
Timing & Persistence
Follow up → every 2–3 days, not once.
Expect → 7–10 touches before a reply.
Silence → means "later," not "no."
